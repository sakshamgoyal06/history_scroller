Study mode

Tap the right side of a post for the next slide, the left side to go back. Scroll for the next post. Content is sample content for the prototype, not today's news.

One app. A calm feed, and a study mode.

Curious mode is what you open instead of Instagram: five good stories, then it ends. UPSC mode is the same habit with the exam switched on: today's current affairs in four short steps, and any topic explained the way UPSC asks it.

Both run on one engine (PRD v2, P1–P9). What changes between them is pace, density and tone, so the switch between them is the one moment in the app that gets real animation.

Taken from how aspirants already study: newspaper and notebook paper, black ink, a yellow highlighter on the line that matters, and coloured sticky flags. Each GS paper has its own flag colour, so GS2 and GS3 read apart at a glance.

curiosity

One variable typeface, Anybody, carries both moods. Curious sets it wide and light; Study sets it condensed and heavy (hover to see it tighten). Entering study mode literally tightens the type. Body text is Mukta from Ek Type in Mumbai, which also covers Devanagari for the Hindi UPSC edition. IBM Plex Mono labels dates, GS tags and sources.

Choices we made, and the defaults we refused

  • New paletteThe current app is warm cream with a serif and terracotta, which is also the look most AI-generated apps land on. Curious moves to cool mist and kingfisher; Study gets paper and highlighter. No gradients anywhere.
  • No pop-upsMode is a toggle in the header, not a modal. Sign-in is asked inline at the first save (FR-ACCT-02). "Explain it UPSC-style" opens a full page you can bookmark, not a sheet.
  • No card gridA post is the whole screen. Lists are rows divided by rules. The only things that look like objects are the story plate and the GS flags, because they carry meaning.
  • Words for tabsThree tabs per mode, labelled in words: Today, Explore, Library or Today, Ask, Library. Down from four in the current app (Zones and Learn merge into Explore).
  • Endings, not streaksCurious ends on "That's today's 5" with what you learned; UPSC ends on "Caught up" with a tally by GS paper. No streak counter, no badges (PRD principle 6).
  • Structure is visibleCurious posts follow hook, context, reveal, why it matters, takeaway. UPSC posts show their four steps by name (What happened, Background, Key facts, Why it matters) so aspirants learn where to look.
  • Trust on screenSources sit under every post and story. AI-written explainers say so; cached editor-checked ones say that instead (FR-TRUST-03).
